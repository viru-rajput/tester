O(1), constant: the number of steps stays the same however much data you have. names[500] takes one step with 10 names or 10 million, because Python jumps straight to that position. A set lookup is O(1) on average for a similar reason: it computes where the item should be and checks only that spot.
O(n), linear: the steps grow in step with the data. Searching an unsorted list for Priya means looking at items one by one, so 10 times more names means about 10 times more work.
O(n²), quadratic: the steps grow with the square of the data. Comparing every item against the others (your 5 × 4 / 2 = 10 case) means doubling the input gives about 4 times the work. 1,000 names took 499,500 comparisons, and 2,000 took 1,999,000.
